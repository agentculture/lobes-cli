# Lightning on Orin

> lobes serves NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B on the Jetson AGX Orin 64GB
> instruction: Run both published recipes verbatim on the physical Orin (c21) before touching any lobes surface; only then wire the winning lane as 'associate'.

## Audience

- The lobes fleet operator running the Jetson AGX Orin 64GB, and every Colleague-mesh caller that addresses a generate lane by role name rather than raw model id.

## Before → After

- Before: The Orin's only local generate lobe above hand is a llama.cpp GGUF cortex measured at 2.61 tok/s -- below its own >=5 tok/s gate, still DECLARED/UNVALIDATED -- and everything faster is a proxy hop to the Spark or Thor. The board declares worker infeasible on a stated reason (W4A4 activations) that is false for this checkpoint.
- After: The Orin serves Nemotron 3.5 Lightning locally as 'associate' -- a lobe that does ground work and hands the result back rather than enacting it -- at a measured, published throughput, with the worker seat left free for a future worker/cortex switch.

## Why it matters

- It converts the mesh's slowest box into one that carries its own ground work instead of proxying it, and it corrects a card-profile claim that is provably wrong about a checkpoint the fleet already serves elsewhere.

## Assumptions

- The vendor's Orin support claim is real but not reproducible from the page. jetson-ai-lab.com/models/nemotron3-5-lightning lists 'Jetson AGX Orin 64GB' as supported, states NVFP4 + vLLM + DSpark at five speculative tokens as the fastest tested configuration, and cites '89 tokens/sec on Jetson AGX Orin across multi-step agentic workloads' -- but its own serve-command generator returns 'No command for this module and engine in model data', so NO Orin command line, image tag, window or utilization is actually published there. Treat 89 tok/s as a vendor claim, not a target: it is an agentic-workload aggregate WITH speculation, not comparable to the repo's own 75.1 tok/s single-stream no-MTP Spark measurement.
- The operator's SECOND reason is the one a responsibilities token cannot serve, and is therefore the load-bearing justification. colleague-stack.md:277-283 says a behaviour difference on an existing lane should be a responsibilities token, and 'associate = worker minus `repo_action`' is exactly that shape -- hand already models the same asymmetry inside an existing role. What a token cannot give is a SEPARATE PUBLIC ADDRESS, which is what keeping the worker seat free for a future worker/cortex switch actually requires.
- Vendor recipes are UNRELIABLE as published and must be verified against the artifact registries before being treated as evidence of support. One of the two recipes named a quantization that does not exist; the jetson-ai-lab page's own serve-command generator separately failed to emit any Orin command at all. This raises the weight on c6 (the 89 tok/s claim is vendor prose, unreproducible from the page) and means a 'supported platform' listing on that site is not by itself evidence the configuration was ever run.
- The frame never stated WHY this could be fast on Ampere, and the stated reason is the wrong one. Every speed hope in the frame is attached to NVFP4/DSpark -- precisely the parts `sm_87` does not accelerate. The load-bearing reason is architectural: Lightning is 30B total with only ~3B ACTIVE per token, so it should decode far faster than the dense 27B this board measured at 2.61 tok/s, on sparsity rather than quantization. If that is the real mechanism, the llama.cpp `Q4_0` lane could succeed even though it discards the vendor's whole NVFP4+DSpark story.
- Nobody in the frame argued the other side: that this box should keep proxying instead. It already reaches worker over the mesh (`WORKER_PEER_ORIGIN` + `WORKER_PEER_PROXY`=true, live in ~/.lobes/.env), and the proxy answer is correct, credentialed and costs this board zero memory. The case for local hosting rests entirely on latency and on not depending on a peer -- neither of which the frame has yet stated as a requirement, and both of which a measured no-go on both lanes would settle in the proxy's favour.
- BOTH halves of the published llama.cpp recipe are now disproven on the platform the page lists as supported: the quantization tag does not exist, and with that corrected the model does not load in NVIDIA's own Jetson-Orin image. This strengthens c28 considerably -- the Orin listing on jetson-ai-lab is very unlikely to have been executed as published, and the '89 tokens/sec on Jetson AGX Orin' figure should not be treated as a reproducible target by this frame.

## Decisions

- BOTH lanes are funded, each run with its published recipe VERBATIM before any lobes-side abstraction. vLLM: vllm/vllm-openai:v0.27.1 with DSpark speculation (nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B-DSpark, 5 tokens), --max-model-len 128000, util 0.7, kv-cache-dtype bfloat16, the five mamba flags. llama.cpp: ghcr.io/nvidia-ai-iot/`llama_cpp`:latest-jetson-orin serving ggml-org/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B-GGUF:`Q4_K_M` with a DFlash draft (-hfd apolo13x/...-DFlash-GGUF, --spec-type draft-dflash), -ngl all -ngld all -fa on. Supersedes q2's earlier vLLM-first-only resolution.
- associate is a full-tier, shedding role ranked between hand and main/cortex (operator decisions, q5+q6). Under pressure it 429s like worker/muse/cortex/senses; hand stays the sole servable floor. Its exact slot among multimodal/worker/muse is unresolved -- see v7.
- The vendor's Jetson recipes are treated as UNVERIFIED DRAFTS, not as evidence of a validated deployment. THREE independent defects across two recipes on the platform the page lists as supported: a llama.cpp -hf tag naming a quantization that does not exist in the repo; a llama.cpp model that fails to load in NVIDIA's own current Jetson-Orin image on a missing Mamba SSM tensor; and a vLLM --`speculative_config`.model naming a Hugging Face repo that does not exist. Each failed at the FIRST thing that would have been caught by running it once. Supersedes the working assumption behind c6 that a 'supported platform' listing implies an executed configuration.

## Hard questions
